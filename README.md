# Is Big Interview Worth It? An Honest Developer’s Take

If you have ever sat in front of your laptop, sweating bullets before a coding interview, you have probably asked yourself: “Why didn’t I prepare more?” Then you open YouTube, watch a 10-minute video about how to “crush your interviews,” close the tab, and go back to Twitter because panic is exhausting.

That is usually when people start looking at interview prep platforms, and one name that comes up often is Big Interview. It markets itself as the end-to-end interview prep platform: mock interviews, behavioral training, video practice, and AI-driven feedback.

Sounds great on paper, but here is the big question: Is Big Interview worth it?

As someone who has gone through more interview prep subscriptions than cups of coffee, let me break down what Big Interview offers, what it does well, where it falls short, and whether it deserves your money, or if another platform (like Educative.io) might be a smarter investment.

---

## What Is Big Interview?

Big Interview is an online platform focused on job interview training. While sites like LeetCode, HackerRank, or Educative teach you the technical skills, Big Interview leans heavily on the soft skills side of the hiring process:

- Mock interview practice with AI feedback
- Video-based behavioral interview prep
- Question banks for technical and non-technical interviews
- Industry-specific content for fields like finance, healthcare, and tech
- Coaching modules to help structure your answers

The idea is simple: most people fail interviews not because they do not know enough, but because they do not know how to communicate their knowledge. Big Interview aims to fix that.

---

## The Pros of Big Interview

Big Interview does some things very well.

### 1. Focus on Behavioral Interviews
Technical prep is everywhere. But behavioral prep is where many developers struggle, especially when a hiring manager asks: “Tell me about a time you disagreed with your team.”

Big Interview guides you through STAR-format answers, helps you stay concise, and forces you to practice instead of improvising.

### 2. Video-Based Mock Interviews
You record yourself answering common questions, and the platform provides feedback. Watching yourself on video can feel uncomfortable, but it is effective. You start noticing filler words, nervous habits, and pacing issues.

### 3. Structured Question Bank
Big Interview provides a large set of behavioral and role-specific questions. If you are new to interviews, this helps eliminate guesswork and lets you focus on practicing real, relevant questions.

### 4. Accessibility Beyond Tech
Unlike platforms designed only for developers, Big Interview covers multiple industries. If you are interested in product management, data roles, business roles, or non-tech fields, it is still useful.

---

## The Cons of Big Interview

Now for the weaknesses.

### 1. Limited Technical Depth
If you are preparing for software engineering interviews, Big Interview will not be enough. It does not cover algorithms, data structures, or system design. You will need a second platform.

### 2. Feels Dated in Some Areas
The UX is functional but not modern. Some of the video modules feel like older corporate training material. Developers may miss the interactivity available on platforms like Educative.io.

### 3. AI Feedback Is Not Human Feedback
The AI critique helps with filler words and body language, but it cannot replace feedback from an actual interviewer. Consider it a starting point, not a complete solution.

### 4. Pricing
Big Interview costs 79 dollars per month or 197 dollars per year. It is not overpriced, but compared with platforms that teach both technical and behavioral preparation, it may feel limiting for the cost.

---

## So… Is Big Interview Worth It?

Here is the straightforward answer: Big Interview is worth it if you struggle with communication and behavioral interviews.

- It is helpful if you freeze up when asked, “Tell me about yourself.”
- It is useful if you want structured interview practice.
- It is worth considering if you are new to the job market and unsure how to describe your experience.

But:

- It is not enough if you are aiming for FAANG-level technical roles.
- It will not teach you system design or algorithms.
- It may feel expensive compared with more comprehensive learning platforms.

The summary: Big Interview is worth it if your biggest weakness is talking about what you can do. If your weakness is actually performing under technical pressure, you will need more than this.

---

## The Technical Gap

No matter how polished your STAR stories are, if you cannot solve the coding challenge, you will not get the job. Big Interview can make you confident, but confidence does not help when you are holding a whiteboard marker and suddenly forget how a hash map works.

This is where platforms like Educative.io become essential.

## Who Should Use Big Interview

Here is the profile of someone who will benefit most from Big Interview:

- Career changers who need practice telling their story
- New graduates with no interview experience
- People who fail behavioral rounds despite passing technical ones
- Non-technical professionals preparing for traditional interviews

If this sounds like you, Big Interview is useful.

---

## My Experience With Big Interview

When I first tried Big Interview, my reaction was, “This is slightly cheesy but helpful.” Watching myself answer “Tell me about a time you failed” was uncomfortable, but it helped me eliminate filler words and tighten my responses.

However, when preparing for a backend developer role, Big Interview was not enough. I still had to rely on Educative.io and LeetCode for technical practice. Big Interview adds polish, but it does not build the core technical skills required for developer interviews.

---

## Final Verdict: Is Big Interview Worth It?

Here is the final conclusion.

- Yes, Big Interview is worth it if you need help with communication, confidence, or behavioral interview preparation.
- No, Big Interview is not enough on its own for developer interviews. You will need a technical platform alongside it.
- The best strategy is using Big Interview for behavioral improvement and Educative.io for technical mastery.

Interviewers are evaluating two things:

1. Can you do the job (technical)
2. Can you explain how you will do the job (behavioral)

Big Interview helps with the second. Educative helps with the first. If you want to land a technical role, you need both.

So, is Big Interview worth it? Yes, but only if you understand what it does and what it does not do. If your technical skills are already strong and you only need polish, it is useful. If you still need system design, algorithm, or practical coding preparation, your time and money are better spent on Educative.
